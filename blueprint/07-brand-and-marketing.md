# 7. Branding and marketing strategy

> Covers deliverable 11 (Branding and marketing strategy) and strategic responsibilities 2 and 10.
> Principle: **local trust beats national reach.** At this stage, marketing exists to create conversations with owners in two niches within driving distance. Everything else is secondary.

---

## 7.1 Brand identity

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Working name *Ostler*; complete the checks in `blueprint/01` §1.4 |
| **Promise** | "Practical AI that pays its way." |
| **Personality** | Dependable, plain-speaking, warm, quietly expert. A trusted local tradesperson for AI, not a Silicon Valley start-up. |
| **Voice rules** | Talk in the customer's numbers (calls, covers, bookings, hours). One idea per sentence. No hype words ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "unleash"). Never imply AI replaces people; it takes the repetitive work. |
| **Visual identity** | Deep green or navy with a warm accent (amber); a clean sans-serif (e.g. Inter or Manrope); simple line icons; **real photos of local clients and the founder**, never robot imagery or stock "AI brains". |
| **Proof assets** | Founder photo and story, client logos (with permission), case study cards with one hard number each, Google reviews, Cyber Essentials badge, "AI used responsibly" trust page |

**Cost:** £0–£300 (DIY in a design tool, or a freelance designer for a logo and template pack).
**Success measure:** five target customers can repeat back what Ostler does in one sentence after seeing the homepage for 10 seconds.

## 7.2 Website

### What and why

Your website is where prospects check you out after a visit, a referral or a LinkedIn post. It must say **what you fix, for whom, at what price, with what proof**, and make booking a free snapshot effortless.

### Structure

| Page | Purpose | Key content |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Clarity in 5 seconds | Headline: "Never miss another customer. Practical AI for [Town]'s local businesses." Three outcomes; logos; one case study; book-a-snapshot button |
| **Industry pages** (one per niche, e.g. `/restaurants`, `/estate-agents`) | Speak the niche's language; rank locally | Niche problems, demo video or **live demo number**, niche case study, package recommendation, FAQs |
| **Services and pricing** | Transparency builds trust and pre-qualifies leads | The four packages with "from" prices, what's included and what isn't |
| **How it works** | Remove fear | Five steps: Snapshot → Assessment → Build → Go-live → Monthly results |
| **Case studies** | Proof | Problem → solution → measured result → quote |
| **ROI calculator** | Interactive lead magnet | "How much are missed calls costing you?" (see `templates/roi-calculator.md`) |
| **Trust and responsible AI** | Answer compliance questions before they're asked | Data hosting, GDPR, human handover, AI disclosure, security, sub-processors |
| **Training** | Second entry point | Courses and dates |
| **About** | Local human face | Founder story, why local business, photo |
| **Book a free AI snapshot** | Conversion | Cal.com embed, 5 qualifying questions |

**Must-haves:** the live AI chat assistant on the site (dogfooding: it *is* the demo), a click-to-call demo number, fast mobile loading, LocalBusiness and Service schema, Google Analytics 4 with conversion events, a privacy policy and cookie consent.

**Tools:** Webflow, Framer or WordPress; Cal.com; GA4; Google Search Console. **Cost:** £0–£40/month plus your time.
**Success measure:** at least 3% of website visitors book a snapshot; industry pages rank top 10 locally for "[niche] AI [town]" within six months.

## 7.3 Google Business Profile (GBP)

- **What:** a fully optimised GBP as "AI consultant" or "Business management consultant" in your town. Choose the category carefully and check what local competitors use.
- **Why:** local businesses search Google Maps for suppliers, and reviews build trust fast. It is also a credibility check: you are selling review management, so your own profile must be exemplary.
- **How:** complete every field; add services with prices; post weekly (tips, case studies, events); upload real photos; ask **every** satisfied client and workshop attendee for a review via a short link; reply to every review within 48 hours.
- **Cost:** free. **Measure:** 10 reviews by month 6 and 25 by month 12; at least 4.8 average rating; monthly calls and website clicks from GBP.

## 7.4 LinkedIn

- **What:** a founder-led personal profile (more reach than company pages) plus a company page.
- **Why:** owners of estate agencies, clinics, hotels and professional firms are active on LinkedIn. It is also where partners (web agencies, accountants, MSPs) are found.
- **How:**
  - Optimise the profile. Headline: "I help [niche] businesses in [region] stop missing customers with practical AI | Founder, Ostler".
  - **Post three times a week** using a 4-part rotation:
    1. A teardown: "I called 10 [niche] businesses at 7pm; here's what happened" (anonymised)
    2. A client win with one number
    3. A practical AI tip for owners
    4. Behind the scenes or a local event
  - Connect with **20 target owners and 10 partners a week** in your region; personalised notes, no pitch on connect.
  - After a genuine interaction, offer the free snapshot.
- **Tools:** LinkedIn (free; Sales Navigator ~£80/month optional from month 3 [A]). **Cost:** time, about 3 hours a week.
- **Measure:** 1,000 relevant local connections by month 6; at least 2 snapshot bookings a month from LinkedIn.

## 7.6 Partnerships (high-leverage channel)

| Partner type | What they get | What Ostler gets | Terms (suggested) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Web design and digital agencies** | White-label AI add-on for their clients; extra revenue | Warm introductions; delivery volume | 15% recurring referral commission for 12 months, *or* white-label wholesale pricing (~30% below list) |
| **Managed IT providers (MSPs)** | A trusted specialist for front-of-house AI; protects their account | Referrals; security credibility | Mutual referral; 10–15% commission |
| **Accountants and bookkeepers** | Value-add for their clients; productivity stories | Trusted introductions to owner-managers | Referral fee, or free AI training for their staff |
| **Vertical software vendors and resellers** (booking, EPOS, PMS) | Implementation partner for AI features | Listing in their partner directory; leads | Apply for partner programmes; certification |
| **Business support organisations** (growth hubs, councils, enterprise agencies, universities) | Expert speaker or workshop provider for SME programmes | Credibility; access to cohorts of SMEs | Paid workshops or fee-free talks for lead generation |

**How:** list 30 potential partners. Meet 5 a month. Offer a free lunch-and-learn for their team. Give them a one-page referral kit and a demo link. Track referrals in the CRM with a partner field.
**Disclosure:** when you pay or receive referral fees, disclose them to clients.
**Measure:** 5 active partners by month 6; 25% of new clients from partners by month 12.

## 7.7 Free AI business assessments and live demonstrations

- **Free AI Opportunity Snapshot:** the primary call to action everywhere. Thirty minutes, with a one-page output. Its real job is to **qualify the prospect and earn the paid assessment or a proposal**.
- **Live demo numbers:** one per niche ("Call 0XXXX XXXXXX to hear Ostler's AI receptionist book a table at a demo restaurant"). Put the number on leaflets, LinkedIn posts, the website and event slides. **This is the most persuasive marketing asset you have.**
- **Personalised demos:** before a second meeting, build a 30-minute "your business" demo trained on the prospect's own website FAQs. This converts very well because owners hear their own business answered.

**Cost:** demo usage £50–£100/month. **Measure:** demo-to-proposal rate of at least 60%.

## 7.8 Case studies

- **Format:** one page and a 60–90 second video. Business, problem (with baseline number), what we built, result after 60–90 days (with number), owner quote, photo.
- **Rules:** written permission; real numbers from the monthly report; no exaggeration; anonymise if requested ("independent 80-cover restaurant in [county]").
- **Distribution:** website, LinkedIn, GBP posts, proposals, events, partner kits.
- **Measure:** two case studies per launch niche by day 120; every proposal includes a relevant one.

## 7.9 Referral programme

- **Client referral offer:** "Refer a business owner. When they go live, you get one month free, and they get £250 off setup." Simple, generous and easy to explain.
- **Ask at peak satisfaction:** at the 30-day results review, ask "Who else do you know who has the same problem?" and request a direct introduction by WhatsApp or email.
- **Measure:** 20% of new clients from client referrals by month 12; referral asks made at 100% of results reviews.

## 7.10 Industry-specific campaigns

Run one campaign per launch niche each quarter. Example: **"The 7pm Test" (hospitality)**

1. **Research:** mystery-call 50 local restaurants at peak time (7–8pm Friday) or after hours. Record answered or unanswered, and whether there was voicemail or a booking link. Use publicly available numbers only. **Never make fake bookings or waste staff time:** if someone answers, ask a quick genuine question (e.g. opening hours), thank them and end the call.
2. **Content:** publish anonymised aggregate results ("31 of 50 calls went unanswered") as a LinkedIn post, a local press release and a leaflet. *Only publish figures you actually measured.*
3. **Outreach:** visit or call the businesses that missed the call, with a respectful, helpful message: "We rang at 7pm last Friday as part of a local study and couldn't get through. Here's what missed calls might be costing, and a free way to check."
4. **Offer:** free snapshot plus a demo, with a pilot slot available.
5. **Measure:** contacts reached → snapshots → pilots.

**Equivalent campaigns:** "The 9pm Enquiry Test" (estate agents: send a portal enquiry at 9pm and measure response time); "The Voicemail Test" (trades); "The Monday 8:30 Test" (clinics: call at opening rush).

## 7.13 How marketing success will be measured

| Funnel stage | Monthly target by month 6 (realistic) | Conversion assumption [A] |
|---|---|---|
| New conversations (calls, visits, events, LinkedIn) | 60 | — |
| Snapshots booked | 12 | 20% of conversations |
| Paid assessments or proposals | 6 | 50% of snapshots |
| New clients | 2 | 35% of proposals |
| Cost per snapshot | ≤ £60 | — |
| Share of clients from referral and partners | ≥ 40% by month 12 | — |

Review weekly (activity) and monthly (conversion). Double down on the two channels with the lowest cost per client won, and cut the worst-performing channel each quarter.
