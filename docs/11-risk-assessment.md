## The three risks that matter most

1. **Founder bottleneck (16).** This is the failure mode of nearly every solo consultancy. The financial model shows it biting by month 4–6 in the Realistic case. Mitigation isn't optional: build the contractor bench before you need it.
2. **No early clients (15).** Momentum and confidence matter. Your warm network is the fastest route to the first client. Use it in week 3, not after the website is "perfect".
3. **Commoditisation (12).** Simple automations and chatbots will keep getting cheaper. The durable value is in **measured outcomes, safe integration with real systems, and keeping things running.** The business is positioned there on purpose.

## Assumptions in the original brief that we've challenged

| Brief assumption | Our challenge | Our response |
|---|---|---|
| Serve ten customer segments | Too broad for a new firm; diluted message | One beachhead (B2B SaaS), one secondary (professional firms), two channels (agencies, VCs) |
| Offer the full service portfolio from day one | A long menu converts worse and spreads delivery thin | Lead with 4 products; the rest is capability, sold on request |
| "Think like McKinsey, Accenture, BCG, OpenAI" | Big-firm models (leverage pyramids, long programmes) don't suit a startup consultancy | Senior-led, productised, fixed-price, small team + contractor bench |
| Build AI products and SaaS platforms | Building products before validated demand burns cash and focus | Productise only after the **same** solution has been delivered 3+ times (see growth strategy) |
| Enterprise as a target | Long cycles, procurement, certifications | Year 2, after case studies and Cyber Essentials Plus / ISO 27001 |
| Equity-based work for early founders | Cash-flow risk, rarely pays off | Cash-paying clients only; equity only as a top-up |
