## 14.1 First-year growth strategy (months 4–12)

### Quarter 2 (months 4–6): Prove repeatability
- **Goal:** 4–6 total clients, 2–3 Builds delivered, MRR £3k–£5k, 2 published case studies.
- Double down on whichever channel produced the first clients (usually warm network + partners).
- Raise prices 10% after the third case study.
- Bring in the **contract delivery lead** as soon as founder load stays above 100% (the model says around months 4–6).
- Achieve Cyber Essentials; begin Plus.
- Launch the **monthly newsletter** and the **AI Opportunity Scorecard** lead magnet.
- Start the **secondary ICP** (professional firms) with a Governance Sprint + document automation offer.

### Quarter 3 (months 7–9): Productise the winners
- **Goal:** MRR £5k–£8k; a repeatable "signature" Pilot (most likely AI support automation for SaaS, or document processing for professional firms).
- Turn the most-sold Pilot into a **productised package**: fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline, standard eval set, standard dashboard. Target 30% fewer delivery days than the first instance.
- Formalise the **partner programme** (tiered referral fees, co-marketing kit, white-label pricing).
- Second fractional Head of AI client (cap at 3).
- Publish a **benchmark report** from anonymised, aggregated project data (with client permission): a strong PR and content asset.

### Quarter 4 (months 10–12): Build the base for year 2
- **Goal:** ~£10k MRR (Realistic case), 8–12 total clients, documented playbooks for every offer.
- First salaried engineer if the hiring triggers in [08-operating-model-and-sops.md §8.3](08-operating-model-and-sops.md) are met.
- Begin **enterprise readiness**: Cyber Essentials Plus done, ISO 27001 gap analysis, security questionnaire library, references.
- Run a **year-1 review with every client**: value delivered, renewal, next workflow, referral.
- Set year-2 targets: **£350k–£600k revenue, 40% recurring** [Estimate].

## 14.2 How the business scales without depending on the founder

The founder must move from **doing → designing → leading**. Each stage removes a dependency.

| Dependency | How it's removed | By when |
|---|---|---|
| Founder delivers every project | Delivery Kit + productised Pilots + delivery lead + salaried engineers; founder reviews architecture only | Months 6–15 |
| Founder runs every Sprint | Sprint playbook (interview guides, scoring model, report template) taught to a senior consultant; founder joins only the readout | Months 9–18 |
| Founder is the only seller | Documented sales playbook with proven conversion rates; partners generating ≥ 30% of leads; BD hire when MRR ≥ £30k | Months 12–24 |
| Founder holds all client relationships | Named delivery lead per account; quarterly reviews co-led | Months 9–18 |
| Knowledge in the founder's head | SOPs, runbooks, recorded walkthroughs, internal knowledge assistant (built on our own stack) | Continuous |
| Founder's personal brand is the only brand | Team members publish; case studies and benchmark reports carry the company brand | Year 2 |

**Test of founder independence (year 3 target):** the founder can take four weeks off and revenue, delivery quality and pipeline don't drop.

## 14.3 Long-term scalability options (years 2–5)

Pursue in this order. Each stage must be proven before the next.

### Stage 1: Managed services as the core (year 2)
- Target **40–50% of revenue recurring** from Run retainers, fractional advisory and AI governance services.
- Recurring revenue lifts valuation multiples and smooths cash flow.

### Stage 2: Vertical playbooks (years 2–3)
- After delivering the **same** solution **3+ times** in one vertical (e.g. document automation for accountancy firms), package it as a **vertical solution**: pre-built connectors, eval sets, compliance documentation, fixed price.
- Sell through that vertical's associations, software vendors and events.
- Each vertical playbook can be delivered by a trained team, not the founder.

### Stage 3: Productised IP (years 2–4, only with evidence)
- **Rule: don't build a SaaS product until (a) 3+ clients have paid for the same custom solution, (b) at least 5 more have asked for it, and (c) margin analysis shows recurring software beats service delivery.**
- Likely candidates (to validate, not assume): the eval & monitoring dashboard from the Run service, or a vertical document-processing product.
- Fund productisation from consulting profits, not outside capital, until product-market fit signals are clear.

### Stage 4: International expansion (year 2+)
- **First markets:** Ireland and the Netherlands/Nordics (English-speaking business culture, EU AI Act demand for governance), then the US East Coast through agency partners.
- Lead with **remote delivery + EU AI Act governance expertise**. A UK firm that understands both UK and EU regimes is a differentiator.
- Price in EUR/USD at local market levels; contract under English law where possible; check local data-protection and tax obligations.

### Stage 5: Team and leverage model (years 3–5)
- Target shape at ~£1.5m–£3m revenue: founder (CEO), Head of Delivery, 2–3 pods (lead + 2–3 engineers each), BD lead, operations. Contractor bench for peaks.
- Healthy consultancy benchmarks to aim for: 70–80% billable utilisation for delivery staff, 20–25% EBITDA, < 20% of revenue from the largest client. [Estimate: typical professional services targets]

## 14.4 Strategic optionality

A focused, profitable, recurring-revenue AI implementation firm with documented IP and vertical playbooks keeps several doors open:
1. **Lifestyle-profitable independent firm** (high margins, founder works less over time).
2. **Scale with debt or revenue-based financing** for hiring, without dilution.
3. **Acquisition target.** Global consultancies and model vendors have recently bought UK AI implementation firms (Faculty by Accenture, Tomoro by OpenAI, both reported in 2026 [Verified]). Buyers pay for **recurring revenue, repeatable IP, a team that delivers without the founder, and strong client references**. Those are exactly the assets this plan builds.
